# Recording directions and scripts

Prepared October 8, 2026 for Daniel Aguilar's actual four demos. Read [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) first: implementation exists, but required personal evidence remains incomplete. These scripts describe features you must demonstrate, not observations to claim before testing.

## Required format

The [professor's assignment](https://vr26.vn.ugavel.com/PortfolioProject1/) requires one narrated **3–6 minute YouTube video per demo**, public or unlisted, showing you operating the running application. A voice-only recording, silent gameplay, slides or a long code walkthrough is not compliant. For the three immersive demos, headset footage and a live-action view of you must be visible together **throughout** the final video. Desktop-only Demo 1 does not require that VR inset. Aim for about 4:30 for Demo 1 and 5:15 for each VR demo; actions take time, so rehearse with a timer.

Speak naturally while operating the app. Use this as a rehearsal guide, not a substitute for understanding. Bracketed measurements must be replaced by what you actually observed. Remove any sentence you cannot support and fix/retest the corresponding requirement before submission.

## A simple Mac + Quest + phone workflow

### Demo 1

1. Open the deployed ride in a normal foreground desktop browser. Keep its controls/readouts visible, hide unrelated tabs/notifications and turn off music.
2. Press **Shift–Command–5**, choose screen or selected-portion recording, and choose your microphone under Options. Record a 10-second test and replay it to check clear narration. Apple's [screen-recording directions](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102618) cover these controls.
3. Record yourself narrating and operating the ride. No separate voice memo is needed.

### Demos 2, 3 and 4

1. First make a **10-second capture test** on the school's actual Quest. If permitted, use the Quest Camera app/Home camera control to record the headset view. Use landscape capture if available. Check game audio and narration in the resulting file; if an Include Mic Audio option is offered, enable it, but still replay the test. Meta documents [native Camera capture and Mac file transfer](https://developers.meta.com/vr/resources/video-capture-mr-vr/).
2. Put your phone or a webcam outside your clear play area, landscape, showing your head/headset, upper body and both controllers. Start that recording too. Let it capture your spoken explanation continuously as a narration backup. Do not use a still photograph as the inset.
3. At the start of both recordings, say “Demo two, sync” (or three/four) and make a visible controller movement. Use the sound/gesture to line up the two clips. Trim the synchronization section from the final video.
4. Operate the actual installed Unity app for Demo 2; enter immersive VR at the public URLs for Demos 3/4. Speak while using the controls. Unplug USB before swinging, wear wrist straps and keep the phone/tripod out of reach. Stop if uncomfortable.
5. After recording, reconnect USB and approve the headset's permitted access/debugging prompt. Meta's Developer Hub File Manager can transfer native recordings to a Mac; native Camera recordings are normally in `Oculus/VideoShots` under shared device storage. If using authorized ADB, list the directory first and pull only the named recording to a local recording folder. Do not expect macOS Finder to mount the Quest like a USB disk.
6. Import both clips into iMovie. Put headset footage on the main timeline and drag the live-action clip above it. Select the overlay clip, open Video Overlay Settings and choose **Picture in Picture**; resize/move it so it does not hide labels, measurements or targets. Extend it from the first to last frame of the final demo. Follow Apple's [picture-in-picture guide](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/guide/imovie/mova1aaa682b/mac).
7. Use one clear narration track; mute duplicated speech to avoid echo, but retain the game/impact audio for Demo 2. Check synchronization after every cut. Export, watch the entire final file and check that the inset never disappears. A headset capture can be 30/36 FPS even when rendering at 72/90 Hz; do not use file FPS as performance evidence.

If Camera/casting/capture is hidden or blocked by school policy, ask the professor/IT for an approved capture method. Do not remove ArborXR or bypass its restrictions. ArborXR's [own quick-menu recording feature](https://help.arborxr.com/en/articles/8028482-capture-screen-recordings-in-arborxr-home-or-kiosk-mode) lists supported PICO/HTC devices, not Quest; do not assume that menu exists on your Quest 3. An approved cast plus a recorded live-action webcam is an alternative, but verify its audio and full-video inset in a short test first. MQDH recording options vary; do not assume a silent/debugging capture contains the required Demo 2 audio.

Use readable resolution and clear audio; cinematic edits/music are unnecessary. Upload **four separate videos** as public/unlisted, not private. Test the actual YouTube watch links in a signed-out browser. Supply the four links for the root table and individual READMEs. A final exported video alone is not the submitted link.
