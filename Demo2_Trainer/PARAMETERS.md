## Validation actually performed

`PortfolioBuild.ValidatePhysics()` numerically measures a 30 cm drop with drag and the configured restitution, verifies spin changes outgoing bounce velocity, and verifies a ball segment crossing 0.6 m cannot skip the paddle. The output is committed in `Validation/physics-results.json` (23.004 cm rebound in the independent numerical check).

`RuntimeVerification.Run()` exercised the real generated scene in Unity play mode, including `Physics.SphereCastNonAlloc` against the actual table collider. The runtime measured **23.054 cm**, compared with the ITTF's approximately 23 cm reference. The small difference between the two checks comes from collision clearance and hit-fraction processing.

These checks validate a published bounce benchmark and specific contact invariants. They do **not** validate every aerodynamic trajectory, friction value or real controller swing. Repeat the in-app bounce check on Quest and show the readout in your video. For stronger evidence, film a real 30 cm drop on an actual table and compare it; enter only an observation you made.

`PortfolioBuild.ValidatePaddleGrip()` checks handle/palm coincidence, shaft/blade axes and render/physics transform agreement for three orientations, plus six high-speed sweeps across both faces. Its output is `Validation/paddle-grip-results.json`. These are transform/contact checks, not proof the hold feels comfortable on a physical controller.
